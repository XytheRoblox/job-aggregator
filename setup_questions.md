# Setup Questions

Welcome! These questions help the job agent learn what you're looking for, so it can score job postings for you. It takes about **5 minutes**.

There are no wrong answers, and **you can change any answer later**. If a question doesn't apply or you haven't decided yet, just say **"skip"** or **"not sure"**.

---

### 1. What's your name?

**Why it matters:** used when helping you prepare applications. It isn't part of the Job Score.

**Example:** Jane Doe

---

### 2. What job titles are you looking for?

**Why it matters:** title fit is the biggest part of the Job Score. List several titles, since companies often call the same job by different names.

**Example:** Product Manager, Product Owner, Technical Product Manager

---

### 3. What levels of seniority would you consider?

**Why it matters:** jobs are scored on whether their level matches yours. Include a level below or above if you'd consider it, since the same title can mean different levels at a small startup and a big company.

**Example:** Mid, Senior, and Staff if the role is a great fit

---

### 4. What industries or areas of work interest you?

**Why it matters:** jobs in these areas score higher. Broad answers are fine, since the agent recognizes related wording (for example, "fintech" also covers payments and banking software).

**Example:** Fintech, Developer Tools, Healthcare

---

### 5. Where would you like to work? List your options from most to least preferred, and anything you'd never consider.

**Why it matters:** jobs that match an option higher on your list get more points. Ranking only affects the order: a job that matches none of your options still appears, just lower. **Only your "never consider" list hides jobs**, so keep that list short. Say whether each option is remote, hybrid or on-site.

**Example:**
- Ranked: 1. Remote (US), 2. Austin, TX (hybrid or on-site), 3. Remote (non-US company, working from the US), 4. Anywhere in the US (hybrid or on-site)
- Never consider: Hybrid or on-site outside the US

---

### 6. Do you need an employer to sponsor a work visa?

**Why it matters:** this is used only to flag jobs whose posting says they don't sponsor visas, so you can skip them. It never lowers the score. Answering is optional.

**Example:** No

---

### 7. What's the lowest yearly salary you'd accept?

**Why it matters:** jobs are scored on whether their pay reaches this amount. Give your walk-away number, not your target. Setting it too high can hide good jobs. Jobs that don't list pay aren't penalized.

**Example:** $120K

---

### 8. What must a job offer for you to consider it?

**Why it matters:** jobs get points for confirming these and lose points for contradicting them. Keep the list short. Most postings don't mention things like these, so they'll often show as "unknown".

**Example:** Health insurance, a clear path to promotion

---

### 9. Is there anything that would rule a job out completely?

**Why it matters:** any posting that matches one of these is automatically marked **Skip**, however well it scores otherwise. Use them sparingly.

**Example:** More than 50% travel, unpaid trial work

---

### Last step: add your resumes

The agent creates a private resumes folder and opens it for you. Drop in one or more resumes (Word, PDF, or text), then tell the agent when you're done.

- **No naming or labeling needed.** If you have several versions (for example, one for product roles and one for design roles), add them all.
- **At least one resume is required**, because matching you to jobs depends on it. This step can't be skipped.
- Your originals are kept as they are. The agent makes readable copies of Word and PDF files.
